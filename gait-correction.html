Goal

Eliminate lateral drift in a hexapod's straight-line gait using reinforcement learning — no manual servo tuning

Role

Programmer

Skills

Outcome

3× reduction in lateral drift · 63% of runs within 5 cm of centerline · convergence in 87 episodes

The SpiderPi hexapod consistently veered left due to small mechanical asymmetries — servo calibration offsets that couldn't be corrected by hand without introducing new errors. The goal was to teach the robot to correct its own drift without any manual tuning, using only onboard sensor feedback and a learned policy. We framed this as a reinforcement learning problem: a Q-learning agent that observes yaw error and selects stride timing adjustments in real time.

THE APPROACH

The SpiderPi hexapod robot has six legs and a built-in tripod gait, but ours consistently veered left due to small mechanical asymmetries — stiffness differences, micro-offsets in servo calibration. Rather than attempting to measure and compensate each discrepancy by hand, we framed the problem as a reinforcement learning task. A Q-learning agent observes the robot's yaw drift and selects corrective adjustments to the stride timing. Over training episodes the agent learns which adjustments produce the straightest paths.

State Space
Discretized yaw error bins (left heavy, centered, right heavy) derived from IMU readings sampled after each stride cycle.
Action Space
Three servo timing adjustments: increase left-side dwell, increase right-side dwell, or maintain current timing. Applied per stride cycle.
Reward
+1 for a stride that reduces yaw error, −1 for one that increases it, 0 for no significant change. Sparse signal kept the agent from overfitting to noise.
Training
Run on the physical robot across multiple 2-meter corridor trials. Q-table updated in real time after each stride; ε-greedy exploration annealed from 0.9 → 0.05 over 200 episodes.

RESULTS

After training, the robot's lateral drift dropped dramatically. The corrected gait held a straight heading across repeated 2-meter runs with no manual tuning between trials — a result that purely hand-tuned compensation had never achieved.

×3 reduction in lateral drift vs. uncorrected baseline
63% of runs finished within 5 cm of centerline over 2 m
32 CM max drift in baseline vs. ~10 cm after training
87 training episodes to convergence
